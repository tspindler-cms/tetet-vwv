The Martin B-57G was the night-attack conversion of the B-57B Canberra, produced under Project Tropic Moon III. Sixteen aircraft were fitted with a new nose carrying forward-looking radar, low-light television, infrared, and a laser designator. A crew could search the Ho Chi Minh Trail in darkness and guide its own bombs. From 1970 to 1972 the 13th Bomb Squadron flew the G from Ubon, Thailand, on night interdiction, typically with incendiaries and laser-guided bombs. It was the Air Force’s first jet bomber built for a self-contained night strike.
